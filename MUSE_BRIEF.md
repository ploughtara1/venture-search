# Venture Search — Master Research Brief

## Objective

Identify genuinely attractive business opportunities that I can develop into a profitable venture.

I am NOT looking for generic startup ideas, AI-wrapper lists, side-hustle articles, or things that merely sound interesting.

The goal is to find opportunities with:

- low initial capital requirements
- realistic ability for one founder to build/test initially
- high potential value to customers
- customers with a real willingness to pay
- recurring or repeatable revenue where possible
- potential to scale beyond selling my own hours
- defensibility or a path toward defensibility
- markets that are not already hopelessly commoditised
- a credible route to first revenue
- a credible path from small experiment to meaningful business

Software, AI, data products, workflow tools, B2B products, specialist marketplaces, automation, information products and unconventional opportunities are all permitted.

Do not restrict the search to my existing industry or expertise.

## Core principle

Do not start with an idea and try to justify it.

Start with problems, markets, unmet demand, inefficient workflows, expensive existing solutions, regulatory changes, fragmented industries, new technology capabilities, underserved niches and emerging behaviour.

Then derive potential businesses from the evidence.

## Research programme

### Phase 1 — Broad opportunity discovery

Search widely across industries and business models.

Investigate sources such as:

- Reddit and specialist communities
- industry forums
- product reviews
- complaints about existing software
- G2 / Capterra-style customer feedback
- app marketplaces
- SaaS pricing
- job advertisements revealing manual workflows
- freelancer marketplaces
- regulatory changes
- industry reports
- Google search demand where evidence is available
- startup/product directories
- failed or abandoned products
- expensive consultants/services that could be productised
- spreadsheet/email/manual workflows
- API and AI capabilities that have recently made something economical to build

Generate a substantial longlist.

Do not filter too aggressively at the beginning.

### Phase 2 — Evidence

For every serious opportunity determine:

1. What exact problem exists?
2. Who experiences it?
3. How frequently?
4. How painful or expensive is it?
5. How is it solved today?
6. What do current solutions cost?
7. What do users dislike about existing solutions?
8. Who would actually pay?
9. Why would they switch?
10. Can a significantly better/simpler/cheaper solution be built?
11. What would the MVP actually contain?
12. Can the MVP be built by one person using modern AI-assisted development?
13. Approximate cost to build and operate it.
14. How would the first 10 customers be acquired?
15. How would the first 100 customers be acquired?
16. What could prevent the business from working?
17. What evidence would falsify the opportunity quickly?

Separate evidence from assumptions.

Cite sources.

### Phase 3 — Economics

Estimate for promising opportunities:

- possible pricing
- gross margin
- recurring costs
- customer acquisition considerations
- realistic number of customers needed for:
  - $1k/month revenue
  - $5k/month
  - $10k/month
  - $25k/month
  - $50k/month
- likely servicing burden
- scalability
- potential for recurring revenue
- potential enterprise value if successful

Do not produce fantasy TAM calculations.

Use bottom-up economics wherever possible.

### Phase 4 — Buildability

Determine:

- MVP architecture
- external APIs/data required
- likely technology stack
- difficult technical components
- regulatory/legal dependencies
- data licensing issues
- expected development effort
- expected operating cost
- what can be automated using AI
- what still requires humans

Prefer businesses where a useful MVP can be produced cheaply and quickly.

### Phase 5 — Competition

For serious candidates map:

- major competitors
- pricing
- target customer
- strengths
- weaknesses
- customer complaints
- feature gaps
- market positioning
- barriers to entry

Do not reject an opportunity merely because competitors exist.

A market with competitors and dissatisfied paying customers may be more attractive than a market with no competition.

### Phase 6 — Validation design

For each finalist specify the cheapest validation experiment possible BEFORE substantial development.

Examples:

- landing page
- customer interviews
- manual concierge service
- prototype
- paid pilot
- pre-order
- outbound campaign
- demo using mocked data
- waitlist
- small advertising test

Specify:

- cost
- effort
- success metric
- failure metric
- next decision

## Evaluation

Assess opportunities across:

- problem severity
- willingness to pay
- accessibility of customers
- competition
- differentiation
- MVP difficulty
- startup cost
- ongoing cost
- speed to market
- recurring revenue potential
- scalability
- founder dependence
- regulatory risk
- data dependency
- distribution difficulty
- defensibility
- upside

Do not hide weaknesses.

An opportunity should survive aggressive criticism before significant money or development effort is committed.

## Important instructions

Research before recommending.

Do not optimise for novelty.

Do not give me a list of trendy ideas with superficial commentary.

Follow evidence wherever it leads.

Actively look for reasons each idea will fail.

Cheap validation is preferred to expensive development.

Do not spend money, purchase subscriptions, contact companies, publish anything, or create paid services without approval.

The desired outcome is not simply an interesting report.

The desired outcome is to identify one or several opportunities worth actually testing, with enough evidence that I can make an informed decision about where to put my time and money.
